The Bug Finding Challenge (BFC) will be held as a team assignment for the Information Security course.

Use AI and security analysis tools to identify vulnerabilities in the provided software, analyze their causes, verify their behavior, and submit a private vulnerability report.

### Submission

Each team must submit **private vulnerability reports** through GitHub.

### Security Report

**Due date:** **Saturday, November 21, 2026 11:59:00 PM KST
URL:** https://github.com/tacose354/KU26_BFC/security

- Use the title format: `[Team 03] Buffer Overflow(brief vulnerability name)`.
- Include the full names of all team members.
- For **each of the Web and System projects**, report **4 in-house code vulnerabilities** and **at least 2 OSS vulnerabilities**, for a total of **8 in-house code vulnerabilities and at least 4 OSS vulnerabilities (at least 12 findings overall)**.
- Follow the **Vulnerability Reporting Template** in the repository’s Security Policy for each finding.
- Include the vulnerable code, root cause, attack path and reachability, reproduction steps, validation results, supporting evidence, security impact, and suggested fix.
- Attach evidence or provide links accessible to the TA. Label each piece of evidence with its corresponding Finding number.

**AI assistance is permitted, but each team must independently verify its findings and clearly distinguish observed results from unverified assumptions.**

Reports must remain private. **Do not disclose findings through public GitHub Issues or Discussions.**


💡 ***Tips for Your Report***

*Even if you cannot reproduce a suspected vulnerability or capture a flag, clearly document your analysis, attempted reproduction steps, observed results, and any limitations.*

*Select the appropriate validation result from the reporting template and explain the evidence supporting your assessment.*

### **BFC Basic Rules and Guidelines**

To ensure a fair and enjoyable experience for all participants, please follow these rules:

1. **Authorized Environments Only**
    
    Conduct security testing only against the designated challenge services and the provided local environments. Do not test unrelated services or systems.
    
2. **No Disruption**
    
    Do not perform excessive scanning or take actions that disrupt the shared servers or other teams’ work. Conduct tests that may crash a service or exhaust resources in your local environment.
    
3. **No Interference with Other Teams**
    
    Do not access, modify, or delete other teams’ data or tamper with the challenge environment.
    
4. **Respect Other Participants**
    
    Treat fellow participants respectfully and follow the course’s academic integrity rules.
    

Failure to adhere to these rules may result in disqualification or other penalties.
